Deriving a batch is a search and costs C(n, k). Checking a batch somebody
claimed is not a search and costs O(claim): do these records exist, do they
belong to this merchant, were they already posted in a prior cycle, do their
signed contributions sum to the credit, does the count match the declaration.
So the check reaches the settlements reconstruction has to refuse, which is
why the composite posts 406 where reconstruction alone posts 104.

The false-alarm row is the one that decides whether this is shippable, and it
is optimistic by construction: the generator's fee model and the verifier's
contribution model are the same model, so a real report that is defect-free
can still disagree with this arithmetic over a fee slab or a rounding
convention, and this benchmark cannot produce that failure. Stated in
LIMITATIONS.md rather than left for a reader to find.

## What the agent did

The agent is a bounded loop over a closed action space: observe the receipt,
choose one action, apply it as an edit to the pipeline's inputs, then re-run the
entire verification stack unchanged over the result. It cannot reach the decision.

```
426  settlements entered the loop as unresolved
166  settled by deterministic triage, with no model call
260  reached the agent
567  actions taken
 32  repaired into a posting, each citing a real record
 50  given a proven cure: verified remedy, deliberately not posted
394  remain held for review
  0  wrong postings caused
```

The triage row is the one worth reading. 39 per cent of the queue is
settled without a model call at all, because a cheap deterministic check
establishes that no action in the vocabulary could change the outcome: the
amounts do not distinguish the transactions, or a rival already appears when the
pool is widened, or there is nothing left to search or tighten. Paying a model to
conclude that nothing can help, on most of a queue, is the same mistake as paying
it to add up a column.

A proven cure is the loop's most common useful output, and it is not a posting.
It is a remediation whose effect has been computed and verified rather than
estimated: tightening this window to seven hours yields exactly one
reconstruction, with the identity closing to zero. It still does not post.
Changing a filter is an assertion about the merchant's settlement behaviour
rather than corroborated evidence, and removing candidates cannot make the
survivor unique, only unexamined. Only an action citing a real record in a real
feed may post.

That rule was learned rather than designed. Without it, an agent able to retune
narrowing produced two wrong postings in three hundred settlements (recorded by
hand from an earlier build, and the only figure in this file not emitted by the
run above, because the build that produced it no longer exists): it tightened
a window, the pool fell from 44 records to 40, an ambiguous settlement became
verified, and the answer was wrong because the tightening had cut real records
out of the batch.

## Which merchants this works for, and why

A single blended auto-post rate across six merchant types describes none of
them. What matters is that the rate is predictable from the amount distribution
alone: one pass over a merchant's historical settlement amounts gives the
contribution spread and the twin mass, those give the collision index, and that
gives the expected status mix. Before any integration, before a single file is
exchanged.

That is only possible because the system knows exactly what makes it fail.

## Does the system know in advance when it is about to be wrong?

This is the real question, and it is more useful than an accuracy figure,
because it says whether the system's own self-assessment is calibrated. The
collision index is computed before any search; the outcome rates are measured
after. If the curves turn where the index predicts they turn, the feasibility
gate and the merchant segmentation are both shippable.

Bands hold roughly equal numbers of configurations rather than equal widths
in log space. Equal widths sound right and put 76 of 96 configurations into
the top two bands, because a handful of points near an index of 1e-4 stretch
the range; the curve then had its coarsest resolution exactly where it was
being asked to say something. Band edges are therefore quantiles of the
observed index, which is less tidy and considerably more honest.

The verified rate is monotone in the index at cardinality 9, and not at 4 and 6.
Where it breaks, it breaks in one direction and for a reason that is a
property of the estimator rather than a defect in the measurement. The
collision index is an EXPECTED number of colliding subsets. At small
cardinality the enumeration is small enough that the realised count is
frequently one where the expectation is five, so the estimator is
conservative precisely where the search is cheapest.

**The wrong-posting rate is zero in every band of every cardinality.** The
breakdown costs recall and never precision, which is the same asymmetry the
rest of the system is built around: a gate that refuses too much is a gate
that is merely expensive.

So the commercial claim is scoped rather than flat. The index predicts a
merchant's auto-post rate at the cardinalities where refusal actually binds,
and under-predicts it below them. That is a narrower claim than the one this
section made before the sweep was segmented, and it is the one the data
supports.

### The two estimators

The published closed form assumes k-subset sums are locally uniform near the
target, with a density read off a moment-matched normal. Real ticket amounts
are close to lognormal, and a sum of three of those is nothing like a normal
distribution in its body. Manhattan therefore measures the density by sampling
the actual pool instead of assuming it, and carries both numbers on every
receipt.

Mean absolute log-ratio against the exhaustively counted number of
reconstructions, across the 57 configurations of 96 where exhaustive
counting was feasible and there is therefore a true count to score
against. The other 39 were refused before enumeration, so nothing was
counted in them and they cannot contribute to an accuracy figure.
0 is perfect, 1.0 is off by a factor of e:

| estimator | error |
|---|---:|
| empirical, by sampling the pool | 1.94 |
| analytic, moment-matched normal | 2.72 |

## Resource envelope, modelled against measured

Publishing a modelled number under a measured heading is exactly the class of
unverified claim this system exists to refuse, so both columns are printed.
Every timing includes the uniqueness proof: these are not solve times with a
proof still owed.

| pool | k | entries predicted | entries observed | MB predicted | MB observed | solve AND prove |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 52 | 7 | 1,943,424 | 1,943,424 | 33 | 30 | 146 ms |
| 52 | 6 | 627,824 | 627,824 | 11 | 10 | 44 ms |
| 100 | 5 | 4,739,872 | 4,739,872 | 81 | 78 | 365 ms |
| 320 | 3 | 1,365,602 | 1,365,602 | 23 | 23 | 86 ms |
| 500 | 3 | 5,208,752 | 5,208,752 | 89 | 89 | 353 ms |
| 1000 | 3 | 41,667,502 | 41,667,502 | 715 | 714 | 3560 ms |

Cost tracks the free cardinality, not the pool size. A 100-record pool at k=5
is more expensive than a 320-record pool at k=3, and that inversion is the
signature of an algorithm matched to the constraint that actually binds.

## What the agent is worth, as a function of how bad the configuration is

The fair criticism of any agent benchmark is that the author created the
problem the agent solves. The answer is a curve rather than an argument: the
batch is re-run with the modelled misconfiguration scaled from zero, and the
agent's contribution is reported at each point.

| scenario | slack | defect rate | verified | wrong | repairs | narrowing | cures | M1 | M1 wrong | B1 wrong |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| correctly configured, reports clean | 0.0 | 0.0% | 120 | 0 | 16 | 0 | 1 | 317 | 0 | 0 |
| correctly configured, reports as modelled | 0.0 | 6.0% | 110 | 0 | 11 | 0 | 0 | 298 | 0 | 22 |
| window misconfiguration as modelled | 1.0 | 6.0% | 73 | 0 | 18 | 7 | 38 | 292 | 0 | 22 |
| window misconfiguration, reports ten times cleaner | 1.0 | 0.6% | 83 | 0 | 20 | 8 | 36 | 312 | 0 | 1 |
| window misconfiguration twice as bad | 1.5 | 6.0% | 51 | 0 | 11 | 0 | 59 | 290 | 0 | 22 |

At zero misconfiguration the corroborated narrowing action repairs nothing,
which is the loop being unnecessary rather than the loop failing. At twice
the modelled misconfiguration it repairs nothing again, for a different and
more interesting reason: a merchant that badly configured proves almost
nothing, never accumulates the twelve proofs a profile requires, and so has
no history to corroborate a window against. The settlements most in need of
the repair are the ones least able to establish the evidence for it.

The wrong-posting column is zero in every scenario, including the ones where
the agent is doing the most work.

The B1 column tracks the report defect rate exactly, which is the point of
varying it: the volume of the composite's advantage depends on a rate this
repository chose, and its existence does not. A reconciliation whose only
check on the report is the report detects a defective one at no rate at all.
