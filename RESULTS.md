## What the agent did

The agent is a bounded loop over a closed action space: observe the receipt,
choose one action, apply it as an edit to the pipeline's inputs, then re-run the
entire verification stack unchanged over the result. It cannot reach the decision.

```
355  settlements entered the loop as unresolved
203  settled by deterministic triage, with no model call
152  reached the agent
296  actions taken
 18  repaired into a posting, each citing a real record
  5  given a proven cure: verified remedy, deliberately not posted
337  remain held for review
  0  wrong postings caused
```

The triage row is the one worth reading. 57 per cent of the queue is
settled without a model call at all, because a cheap deterministic check
establishes that no action in the vocabulary could change the outcome: the
amounts do not distinguish the transactions, or a rival already appears when the
pool is widened, or there is nothing left to search or tighten. Paying a model to
conclude that nothing can help, on most of a queue, is the same mistake as paying
it to add up a column.

A proven cure is the loop's most common useful output, and it is not a posting.
It is a remediation whose effect has been computed and verified rather than
estimated: tightening this window to seven hours yields exactly one
reconstruction, with the identity closing to zero. It still does not post.
Changing a filter is an assertion about the merchant's settlement behaviour
rather than corroborated evidence, and removing candidates cannot make the
survivor unique, only unexamined. Only an action citing a real record in a real
feed may post.

That rule was learned rather than designed. Without it, an agent able to retune
narrowing produced two wrong postings in three hundred settlements: it tightened
a window, the pool fell from 44 records to 40, an ambiguous settlement became
verified, and the answer was wrong because the tightening had cut real records
out of the batch.

## Which merchants this works for, and why

A single blended auto-post rate across six merchant types describes none of
them. What matters is that the rate is predictable from the amount distribution
alone: one pass over a merchant's historical settlement amounts gives the
contribution spread and the twin mass, those give the collision index, and that
gives the expected status mix. Before any integration, before a single file is
exchanged.

That is only possible because the system knows exactly what makes it fail.

### The two estimators

The published closed form assumes k-subset sums are locally uniform near the
target, with a density read off a moment-matched normal. Real ticket amounts
are close to lognormal, and a sum of three of those is nothing like a normal
distribution in its body. Manhattan therefore measures the density by sampling
the actual pool instead of assuming it, and carries both numbers on every
receipt.

Mean absolute log-ratio against the exhaustively counted number of
reconstructions, across 56 swept configurations (0 is perfect, 1.0 is off by a
factor of e):

| estimator | error |
|---|---:|
| empirical, by sampling the pool | 1.93 |
| analytic, moment-matched normal | 2.75 |

## Resource envelope, modelled against measured

Publishing a modelled number under a measured heading is exactly the class of
unverified claim this system exists to refuse, so both columns are printed.
Every timing includes the uniqueness proof: these are not solve times with a
proof still owed.
